# Offer discovery

An offer is a specific outcome for a specific buyer, with a scope and a price
the user has chosen. Your job is to draw it out of the user, not to invent it.

## Process

1. Read what the user already gave you (chat, project instructions, memory,
   attached files). Reuse their words.
2. List what is still unknown from this set: the outcome the buyer gets, who
   buys and who uses, what is included and excluded, how delivery works, what
   proof exists, and what the buyer does today instead.
3. Ask only the one or two unknowns that change the offer most, at the end of
   the reply. Stop and wait.
4. Once answered, write the offer brief to a workspace file.

## Offer brief

- **One-line offer** — "We help [buyer] get [outcome] by [mechanism]."
- **Buyer and user** — roles, company type, trigger that makes them buy now.
- **Outcome** — the result in the buyer's terms, with only metrics the user gave.
- **Scope** — included, excluded, how long, what the buyer must provide.
- **Proof** — real customers, results, or credentials; otherwise "none yet" and
  a note on what to collect.
- **Alternatives** — what the buyer does today, including doing nothing.
- **Open questions** — everything still assumed, marked as assumptions.

## Rules

- Do not make up customers, testimonials, results, or guarantees.
- Offer two or three framings as options when the user is unsure; do not pick
  one silently.
- Price belongs to `pricing-and-terms`; market selection to
  `market-positioning`. Hand off once the brief lands.
